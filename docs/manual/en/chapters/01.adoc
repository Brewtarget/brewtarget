== Chapter 1: A Walkthrough

This chapter takes you through designing a recipe from start to finish, then brewing it.  The example is an English
Best Bitter brewed on a Grainfather G30, but the steps are the same whatever you brew and whatever you brew it on.
Nothing here depends on getting the details right first time: every change can be undone, and you can come back to any
step later.

image::screenshots/mainWindow.png["The main window with the example recipe open",pdfwidth=100%]

=== The Main Window

The main window has three parts.

* On the left is a set of *trees*, one for each kind of thing {app-name} stores: recipes, styles, equipment, mash,
  boil and fermentation profiles, fermentables, hops, miscellaneous ingredients, yeasts and water profiles.  The icons
  down the side switch between them (hover over an icon to see which is which).  Double-click a recipe to open it, or
  any other item to edit it.  You can also drag items onto the recipe to use them there.
* At the top right are tabs about the recipe as a whole: *Recipe* (the main figures), *Extras*, *Notes*, *Water* and
  *Brewday*.  Once you have brewed a recipe, there is also a tab for each brew log.
* At the bottom right are tabs for the parts of the recipe: *Fermentables*, *Hops*, *Miscellaneous*, *Yeast*, *Mash*,
  *Boil* and *Fermentation*.

There is no Save button on the main window.  Changes to a recipe are stored as you make them, and
menu:Edit[Undo] and menu:Edit[Redo] take you backwards and forwards through them.  The editors that open in their own
window (for equipment, hops, mash profiles and so on) are different: they have btn:[Save] and btn:[Cancel] buttons, and
nothing you change in them is kept until you click btn:[Save].

=== Units

{app-name} works internally in metric units, but you can show most things in whichever units you prefer.  Go to
menu:Edit[Options], and choose your units on the *Units & Precision* tab -- eg litres or US gallons for volume, Celsius
or Fahrenheit for temperature, specific gravity or Plato for density.

To change the units for just one field, right-click its label.  You can also type a unit after a number, and it will be
converted -- eg typing `5 gal` into a volume field that shows litres converts it to litres for you.

=== Equipment

Much of what {app-name} calculates -- how much water you need, how much wort ends up in the fermenter, how much beer you
can package -- depends on your equipment, so it is worth setting this up before anything else.

Open menu:View[Equipments].  This lists the equipment profiles in your database, including ready-made profiles for a
number of all-in-one systems (Grainfather, BrewZilla, Braumeister and others) and a basic cooler mash tun set-up.  If
yours is there, select it and click the edit button; otherwise create a new one with the button next to it.

image::screenshots/equipmentEditor.png["The General tab of the Equipment editor",pdfwidth=75%]

The editor has a tab for each vessel, plus a *General* tab and a *Mash* tab.  Most fields have an explanation beneath
them, so this section only covers the ones that matter most.

General tab::
*Default Batch Size* is the volume that goes into the fermenter, and *Default Pre-boil Volume* is what you expect to
have in the kettle before the boil.  A new recipe starts with these, and you can change them per recipe.  (They are not
the capacities of the fermenter and kettle, which are set on those vessels' own tabs.)  The checkboxes at the top
control which of the optional vessel tabs are shown.  If you have no separate mash tun, untick *Mash Tun*: {app-name}
then assumes the mash happens in the kettle, as it does on an all-in-one system or for brew in a bag.

Mash tab::
*Grain absorption* is the water the spent grain keeps hold of.  *Foundation Water* is the water that has to be below the
false bottom (or malt pipe) before the grain is covered, but which drains out with the wort -- about 3.5 litres on a
Grainfather G30.

Boil Kettle tab::
*Evaporation rate per hour* and *Kettle to Fermentor Loss* (the trub, and whatever stays in the chiller and pipework)
have the biggest effect on your volumes.

Fermentor tab::
*Deadspace Losses* is what stays behind with the yeast when you transfer or package the beer.

The figures in the ready-made profiles are the manufacturers' own, which are a reasonable starting point.  After a few
brews, the brew logs will tell you how far your own figures differ.

Tick *Set as Default* if this is the equipment you normally use, so that new recipes pick it up automatically, then
click btn:[Save].

=== Creating the Recipe

Choose menu:File[New Recipe] (or press kbd:[Ctrl+N]), and give the recipe a name.  It appears in the recipe tree and
opens on the Recipe tab.

image::screenshots/recipeTab.png["The Recipe tab of the new recipe, before adding ingredients",pdfwidth=100%]

If you did not set default equipment, choose it from the *Equipment* drop-down.  {app-name} asks whether to set the
recipe's batch size and pre-boil volume from the equipment; say yes unless you are brewing an unusual batch size.

Next, choose a *Style* -- here, Best Bitter.  The style is optional, but it means that the bars for colour, bitterness,
OG, FG and ABV show the style's range, so you can see at a glance whether the recipe fits it.  The styles supplied
include the 2021 BJCP guidelines, and you can add your own with menu:View[Styles].

*Efficiency* is the brewhouse efficiency: how much of the potential extract of the grain ends up in the fermenter.  If
you don't know yours, 70% is a reasonable guess for most systems; the brew log will tell you the real figure after your
first brew day.

=== Fermentables

Click the plus button on the *Fermentables* tab to open the fermentable catalogue.  Type in the filter box to narrow the
list down, select what you want and click btn:[Add to recipe].  You can add several things before closing the
catalogue.  (Alternatively, drag fermentables from the tree on the left onto the recipe.)

For the Best Bitter, add Maris Otter and a medium crystal malt, eg *Caramel/Crystal Malt – 60L*.  New additions start
with no amount, so double-click in the *Amount* column to set one.  As you do, the OG, colour and ABV on the Recipe tab
update.  Aim for something like 92% pale malt and 8% crystal by weight, and adjust the total to put the OG in the middle
of the style's range.

image::screenshots/fermentablesTab.png["The Fermentables tab",pdfwidth=100%]

If something is not in the catalogue, create it with the new-item button at the bottom of the catalogue.  The most
important fields are the yield and the colour, which you can usually get from the maltster's specification sheet.

=== Hops

Hops work the same way: click the plus button on the *Hops* tab, choose the hops and add them to the recipe.  For each
addition, set:

* the *Amount*;
* the *Stage* -- Mash, Boil, Fermentation (for dry hops) or Packaging;
* the *Time*.  For the boil, this counts down to the end of the boil, as in most recipes, so a 60-minute addition goes
  in 60 minutes before the end.  For the other stages, it counts from the start, so a dry hop at 3 days goes in three
  days after fermentation starts.

For the Best Bitter, add Challenger at 60 minutes for bitterness and East Kent Goldings at 15 minutes for flavour and
aroma, and adjust the amounts to get about 35 IBU.  The *IBU/GU* scale on the Recipe tab shows how bitter the beer
will taste relative to its strength.

Check the *Alpha %* column against the packet, as it varies from crop to crop.  Alpha acid belongs to the hop rather
than to the recipe, so changing it -- here or in the hop's editor -- changes it for every recipe that uses that hop.  If
you want to keep the old figure for older recipes, copy the hop first (select it in the tree, then
menu:Edit[Copy Selected Item(s)]) and use the copy.

The IBU figures depend on which formula is used.  The default is Tinseth, and you can choose another on the *Formulas*
tab of menu:Edit[Options].

=== Yeast and Other Ingredients

Add a yeast from the *Yeast* tab -- for a Best Bitter, an English ale yeast such as SafAle S-04.  The yeast's
attenuation is what {app-name} uses to estimate the FG.

The *Miscellaneous* tab is for everything else: finings, spices, fruit and so on.  Water treatment (salts and acids)
also ends up here, but it is easier to let the Water tab work that out for you, as described below.

=== The Mash

The mash profile says how the mash is done: the rests and their temperatures, how much water goes in at each step, and
how the grain is sparged.

On the *Mash* tab, choose a profile from the drop-down.  The ones supplied cover the common cases -- single infusion
mashes for light, medium and full body, step mashes and a decoction mash -- and their names say what they are.  For the
Grainfather, choose *Single Infusion, Medium Body, Fly Sparge*.

The supplied profiles don't have any water amounts or temperatures, because those depend on the recipe.  To fill them
in, click the edit button next to the drop-down to open the mash editor, then:

. Check the *Sparge* method.
. Set the *Target mash thickness* (the volume of strike water per weight of grain).  Grainfather suggest 2.7 L/kg;
  around 2.5--3 L/kg is typical for most systems.
. Click btn:[Auto-Adjust].

image::screenshots/mashEditor.png["The mash editor after Auto-Adjust",pdfwidth=75%]

The supplied profiles are locked, so that they stay available for other recipes.  {app-name} asks whether to make a
copy for this recipe; say yes, and it adjusts the copy instead.  It works out the strike water, sparge water and
temperatures from your grain bill and equipment, so that you end up with the recipe's pre-boil volume.  If anything
doesn't add up -- eg the mash won't fit in the vessel -- it says so.

If you tick *Keep mash in sync with recipe changes*, the mash is adjusted again whenever something it depends on
changes, eg if you add more grain or change the batch size.  This is only possible for a mash that just one recipe
uses.

Click btn:[Save] to close the mash editor.  Back on the Mash tab, the line next to the drop-down tells you whether the
mash is shared with other recipes, and whether it is locked.

*Use mash profile in FG calculation*, on the Mash tab, makes the FG estimate allow for the mash temperatures and
thickness, so that, eg, a hotter mash gives a higher FG.  Without it, the FG depends only on the yeast's attenuation.

=== Water

You can skip this section if you don't adjust your brewing water.

The *Water* tab works out which brewing salts to add, and how much acid, to turn the water you have into the water you
want.  It needs the water amounts from the mash, which is why it comes after the mash in this walkthrough.

. Choose the *Base Profile*: your tap water, or whatever water you brew with.  There are profiles for a few cities, but
  you will probably need to create your own from your water supplier's report, with menu:View[Waters].  If you dilute
  with reverse-osmosis water, set the *% RO* for the mash and the sparge.
. Choose the *Target Profile*.  There is one for each broad family of beer styles; for the Best Bitter, choose
  *Amber, Hoppy (Bitter, ESB, Amber Ale)*.
. If you want acid added to hit a particular mash pH, tick *Adjust acid for mash pH of* and set the pH -- 5.4 is a good
  default.
. Click btn:[Auto-Adjust].

image::screenshots/waterTab.png["The Water tab after Auto-Adjust",pdfwidth=100%]

The salts and acid it chooses appear in the *Water Agents* table and on the Miscellaneous tab.  The figures under
*Actual* show what you will end up with, and the chart compares this with the base and target profiles.  It is rarely
possible to match a target exactly, so the *Auto-Adjust Notes* tab explains any compromises.

As with the mash, you can tick the box to keep the water agents in sync with the rest of the recipe.

=== Boil and Fermentation

A new recipe starts with a standard boil profile: heating up to the boil, the boil itself, and cooling.  Set the length
of the boil in the boil step on the *Boil* tab.  If you do a hop stand or whirlpool, add a step for it after the boil.
Only some IBU formulas (currently mIBU and SMPH) estimate the bitterness from hops added after the boil; the others
treat them as adding none.

Likewise, a new recipe starts with a single primary fermentation step.  On the *Fermentation* tab, set its temperature
and length -- eg 19°C for ten days -- and add more steps if you do, eg, a diacetyl rest or a cold crash.

=== Checking the Recipe

Go back to the *Recipe* tab and check the figures against the style.  The bars show the style's range, with a marker
for the recipe's own value; if a value is outside the range, adjust the ingredients.

image::screenshots/recipeTabDone.png["The Recipe tab of the finished recipe",pdfwidth=100%]

Under *Batch Size*, *Target* is the volume into the fermenter, *Calculated* is what the equipment and recipe actually
give you, and *Packaged* is what you can expect to bottle or keg once the fermenter losses and any dry hops have taken
their share.

=== Brew Day

On the *Brewday* tab, click btn:[Generate Instructions] for a step-by-step list of what to do on the day, worked out
from the recipe.  You can edit, add, remove and reorder the steps.

To print the recipe or the brew day instructions, or save them as PDF or HTML, use menu:File[Print and preview].

When you brew, click *Brew It!* at the right-hand end of the toolbar, or right-click the recipe in the tree and
choose *Brew It!*.  This:

* creates a brew log for today, which opens as a new tab next to the Recipe tab and also appears under the recipe in
  the tree;
* takes the ingredients out of your inventory, if you keep track of it;
* warns you if the mash water doesn't match the recipe -- eg because you changed the grain bill after adjusting the
  mash, and didn't keep it in sync.

image::screenshots/brewLog.png["A brew log",pdfwidth=100%]

As you brew, fill in the brew log: the volumes and gravities you measure, the mash temperatures, the date fermentation
finished and so on.  It calculates the efficiency into the kettle, the brewhouse efficiency and the attenuation, and
shows them next to the figures the recipe predicted.  Use the brewhouse efficiency to correct the efficiency on the
recipe for next time, and any shortfall in volumes to correct the losses in the equipment profile.

Once you have brewed a recipe, you may want to tick *Recipe Locked* on the Recipe tab, so that it can't be changed by
accident.  To brew a variation, copy it and change the copy.
